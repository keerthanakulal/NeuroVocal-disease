Welcome to NeuroVocal AI

This medical intelligence tool utilizes state-of-the-art Acoustic Machine Learning models to analyze vocal features (such as jitter, shimmer, harmonic-to-noise ratio, and MFCC patterns) from vocal cord vibrations.

By examining these voice biomarkers, the system provides predictions regarding potential underlying vocal or neurological health disorders, serving as an effective preliminary screening utility.

How to Start screening

To analyze a patient's voice:

  1. Select the Voice Analyzer from the sidebar.
  2. Have the patient read the standardized text template out loud, or upload a pre-recorded audio file.
  3. Run the classification model to review probability graphs across different diagnostic categories.

Categorical Class Probabilities

Parkinson's Disease (PD) Neurological

Parkinson's disease is a neurodegenerative disorder that impacts motor systems and dopamine-producing neurons. Early vocal symptoms—collectively known as hypokinetic dysarthria—include reduced loudness, vocal tremors, breathiness, and a monotone speech pattern.

Common Acoustic Biomarkers:
  • Reduced vocal cord stability (High Jitter)
  • Unstable vocal intensity (High Shimmer)
  • Monotonic pitch frequency variations

Alzheimer's Disease (AD) Neurological

Alzheimer's is a progressive cognitive illness that destroys memory and mental function. In its early stages, it significantly impacts language processing, leading to word-finding pauses, slower speech articulation rates, and simplified grammar.

Common Acoustic Biomarkers:
  • Higher silent pause frequencies and durations
  • Reduced articulation speed and flat prosody
  • Loss of conversational pitch rhythm

Clinical Stress Psychological / Somatic

Acute or chronic psychological stress increases muscle tension throughout the body, including the laryngeal muscles. This somatic response directly impacts the voice, causing high vocal strain, elevated pitch, and trembling speech.

Common Acoustic Biomarkers:
  • Elevated fundamental frequency (High Mean F0)
  • Increased subglottic tension and vocal instability
  • Vocal tremor and breathy voice dropouts

Clinical Depression Psychological

Depression leads to psychomotor slowing, which reflects clearly in vocal acoustics as flat, dull, breathy, and monotone speech (often referred to as 'flat affect' or vocal flatness).

Common Acoustic Biomarkers:
  • Flat fundamental frequency (Low Std Dev F0)
  • Reduced overall speech rate and prolonged pauses
  • Muted spectral resonance and quietness

Isolate Environmental Noise

Ensure the patient is seated in a quiet room. Microphones pick up low-frequency hums from air conditioners, computer fans, and wall echo reflection, which can negatively bias acoustic jitter calculations.

2

Microphone Placement

Maintain a distance of approximately 10 to 15 centimeters (4 to 6 inches) between the patient's mouth and the microphone. Direct puffing/breathing on the microphone element creates high-amplitude bass spikes that distort mel spectrograms.

3

Vocal Tone & Pace

Instruct the patient to read the text in a comfortable, relaxed, monotone voice. Artificial whispering or forced speaking dynamics alter fundamental frequency (F0) trends, which could skew machine learning classifications.
